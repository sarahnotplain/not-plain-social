# Not Plain: caption voice

Captions are a quiet invitation to read the post. Sarah's writing does the work; the caption points to it.

## Voice

- Plain, spare and specific. Write short sentences, the way a writer would talk about her own work.
- Write in first person as Sarah ("New essay", "I wrote about…"). Don't write about her in third person, except in the Pinterest description.
- Never write anything she didn't write or wouldn't say. If a detail isn't in the post, leave it out.

## Don't

- Use emoji or exclamation points.
- Use em dashes. Use a period or a comma instead.
- Use AI filler: "dive into", "journey", "unpack", "delve", "powerful", "raw and honest", "in this piece", "I'm so excited to share", "a must-read", "let that sink in".
- Use true-crime bait: "shocking", "chilling", "twisted", "the truth about", "what really happened".
- Ask engagement-bait questions ("Have you ever…?").

## What a caption is for

The image already shows her exact words. The caption's job is to make someone curious enough to go read the rest. Plain captions ("New essay. Link in bio.") waste that space.

A good caption:

1. **Hook.** The quote (in quotation marks) or the title, as the first line. This is what shows before "more".
2. **Scene, 2 to 4 short sentences.** Put the reader inside the essay: a place, a moment, a concrete detail or two taken from the post. Write in first person, present or past tense to match the essay.
3. **Open loop.** End the scene on something unresolved that the essay answers, without giving the ending away. ("Nobody except my aunt." "And the one thing she forgot at the house.")
4. **Call to action.** "New essay on Not Plain." (first post) or "From *Title*, on Not Plain." (follow-ups), then "Read it at the link in bio." / "Read it here:" + URL.

Every detail must come from the post itself. Small paraphrases are fine; new facts, feelings or interpretations are not. Don't reveal how the essay ends, and never tease violence ("what happened next will…"). The pull should come from the people and the moment, not from the crime.

Each of an article's three captions should use **different details** from the essay, so someone who sees all three gets three different ways in.

## Per platform

**Instagram:** hook, blank line, scene, blank line, call to action ending "Read it at the link in bio.", blank line, 3 to 5 hashtags on their own line.

**Facebook:** same hook and scene, then the call to action ending "Read it here:" with the full post URL on the next line (Facebook turns it into a link preview). At most 2 hashtags, or none.

**Pinterest** (people find pins through search, so use plain words people would type):

- **Title** (100 characters max): the post title plus a searchable phrase, such as "…: an essay on grief and old family photographs".
- **Description** (500 characters max): 2 to 3 sentences in third person with concrete details from the post and searchable words (memoir, grief, family secrets, narrative nonfiction, true story). End with "From Not Plain, a memoir newsletter by Sarah."

## Follow-up posts (packages 2 and 3)

Each article gets up to three posts so the feed stays active between essays. The first announces it. The others share a different line and point back to it.

- Don't say "New" or "new essay" in follow-ups.
- Lead with the quote, then a scene built from different details than the first post used, then "From *Title*, on Not Plain."
- Keep the same call to action: "Read it at the link in bio." on Instagram, the URL on Facebook.
- Pinterest: use a different title angle for each package so the pins don't compete in search.

## Substack Notes

A note is short and has no scene to borrow, so the caption stays brief and points to the newsletter:

- Hook: the note's words in quotation marks.
- If the note shares one of her essays, one sentence from that essay's world (details from the essay itself), then "From *Title*, on Not Plain. Read it at the link in bio." / "Read it here:" + URL.
- Otherwise: "From my notes on Substack. Not Plain is a memoir in progress. Subscribe to see how it unfolds." ("Not Plain is a memoir in progress. Subscribe to see how it unfolds." is her own line from the end of her posts.) Then "Link in bio." / the URL.
- Don't explain or interpret the note. Let it stand.
