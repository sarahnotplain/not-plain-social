# Not Plain: caption voice

Captions are a quiet invitation to read the post. Sarah's writing does the work; the caption points to it.

## Voice

- Plain, spare and specific. Write short sentences, the way a writer would talk about her own work.
- Write in first person as Sarah ("New essay", "I wrote about…"). Don't write about her in third person, except in the Pinterest description.
- Never write anything she didn't write or wouldn't say. If a detail isn't in the post, leave it out.

## Don't

- Use emoji or exclamation points.
- Use em dashes. Use a period or a comma instead.
- Use AI filler: "dive into", "journey", "unpack", "delve", "powerful", "raw and honest", "in this piece", "I'm so excited to share", "a must-read", "let that sink in".
- Use true-crime bait: "shocking", "chilling", "twisted", "the truth about", "what really happened".
- Ask engagement-bait questions ("Have you ever…?").

## Per platform

**Instagram** (the caption sits under the image):

1. First line: the quote or the title. This is what shows before "more".
2. A blank line, then one or two sentences on what the post is about. Use the subtitle if it's good.
3. "Read it at the link in bio." (Links in captions don't work on Instagram.)
4. 3–5 hashtags on their own line.

**Facebook:**

1. The quote or the title.
2. One or two sentences.
3. The full post URL on its own line. Facebook turns it into a link preview.
4. At most 2 hashtags, or none.

**Pinterest** (people find pins through search, so use plain words people would type):

- **Title** (100 characters max): the post title plus a searchable phrase, such as "…: an essay on grief and old photographs".
- **Description** (500 characters max): 1–3 sentences in third person that say what the post is about using searchable words (memoir, grief, family secrets, narrative nonfiction, true story). End with "From Not Plain, a memoir newsletter by Sarah."

## Follow-up posts (packages 2 and 3)

Each article gets up to three posts so the feed stays active between essays. The first announces it. The others share a different line and point back to it.

- Don't say "New" or "new essay" in follow-ups.
- Lead with the quote, then something like "From There, But Not Here, on Not Plain." One sentence on what the essay is about is fine if it's true to the post.
- Keep the same call to action: "Read it at the link in bio." on Instagram, the URL on Facebook.
- Pinterest: use a different title angle for each package so the pins don't compete in search.
